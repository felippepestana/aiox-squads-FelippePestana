# Onboarding Best Practices

Practical patterns the squad applies across journeys. Onboarding is an **experience**, not a paperwork event.

## Pre-boarding (before day 1)
- Send a warm welcome with practical logistics (start time, where/how, who to ask).
- Provision access/equipment **before** day 1 — nothing kills momentum like a missing laptop.
- Share the 30/60/90 plan so expectations are clear and mutual.

## Day 1
- Make it human first: team intro, buddy intro, a friendly walkthrough — not a form marathon.
- Verify access works; one tiny early win (a successful first commit/query/call) beats a perfect setup.

## Week 1
- Daily light check-ins; assign a real first task with support.
- Buddy handles "silly questions"; manager owns belonging and direction.

## 30 / 60 / 90
- Schedule the review **up front**, not as an afterthought.
- Make feedback two-way: ask the new hire what's working in onboarding (and fix it for the next person).

## Remote / hybrid specifics
- Be explicit about communication norms (async vs sync, response expectations).
- Over-invest in relationships and visibility; isolation is the remote onboarding risk.

## Anti-patterns to avoid
- One-size-fits-all journeys (ignore the role/person).
- Replacing the manager with a tool or a buddy.
- Front-loading paperwork over people.
- Letting milestones slip silently (no proactive follow-up).
- Using personality/behavioral data to judge — it's **context, weight 0**.
