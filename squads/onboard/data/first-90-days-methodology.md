# The First 90 Days — applied to onboarding

Based on Michael Watkins' *The First 90 Days*. The first 90 days set the trajectory: the goal is to reach the **break-even point** (when the new hire contributes as much as they consume) as fast — and as humanely — as possible.

## Core ideas we use

- **Match the journey to the situation (STARS):** a hire into a **Start-up**, **Turnaround**, **Accelerated growth**, **Realignment** or **Sustaining-success** context needs a different first 90. Tailor expectations accordingly.
- **Accelerate learning:** front-load understanding of the product, customers, systems, and culture before expecting output.
- **Secure early wins:** target a concrete, visible win around day ~25–30 to build confidence and credibility.
- **Build relationships & belonging:** a buddy, manager 1:1s, and key stakeholder intros — belonging is a top driver of early retention.
- **Negotiate expectations:** align on what success looks like at 30/60/90 with the manager, in writing.

## 30 / 60 / 90 shape

| Phase | Focus | Outcome |
|-------|-------|---------|
| **0–30** | Learn & connect | Environment/access ready, team met, shadowing, first small task, buddy active |
| **30–60** | Contribute | Owns a recurring deliverable, first stakeholder interaction, ramp gaps closing |
| **60–90** | Own | Independent on the core scope; 30/60/90 review; two-way feedback |

## Principles in this squad
- Personalize to **role objectives + behavioral context** (profiler-dna, **weight 0** — tailors pace/communication, never judges).
- Carry **hiring context** (talent-compass candidate report) so onboarding closes the *right* ramp gaps.
- **Augment the manager**, never replace 1:1s, direction or belonging.
- **Follow up proactively** — onboarding fails silently when no one watches the milestones.
