# OKR Methodology (Objectives & Key Results)

Based on *Measure What Matters* (John Doerr). The whole point: **Objectives** say where you're going (qualitative, inspiring); **Key Results** prove you got there (measurable outcomes).

## Rules that matter

1. **Key results are outcomes, not tasks.** "Launch the dashboard" is a task. "Raise weekly active usage 40% → 60%" is a key result. If a KR has no number that moves, rewrite it.
2. **Every KR has baseline → target.** No metric, no KR.
3. **Ambitious but credible.** Aim high (≈70% attainment is healthy for stretch OKRs); flag sandbagging (always 100%) and vanity metrics.
4. **Line of sight.** Each Objective ladders up to a team/company goal.
5. **Few and focused.** 1 Objective with 3–5 KRs per scope, per cycle.
6. **OKRs ≠ compensation.** Tying pay directly to OKR attainment kills ambition and invites gaming. Keep them advisory input.

## Good vs. bad KRs

| ❌ Task / vanity | ✅ Outcome |
|---|---|
| "Ship onboarding revamp" | "New-user activation 30% → 45%" |
| "Publish 10 blog posts" | "Organic signups 200 → 350/mo" |
| "Do customer interviews" | "Churn reasons mapped → top-3 fixed; logo churn 5% → 3%" |

## Cadence
Weekly or bi-weekly check-ins; score at cycle end (e.g., 0.0–1.0 per KR) as a learning signal, not a verdict.
