# Compensation Benchmarking & Bands — Reference

Used by `comp-strategist`. Standard compensation-design mechanics, kept explainable line by line.

## Building a band

A **salary band** is a pay range for a level within a role family. Three numbers + a spread:

- **Minimum** — entry to the level (new-in-level, still ramping).
- **Midpoint** — the market reference for a fully proficient person at this level.
- **Maximum** — top of the level (deep expertise; beyond this, it's the next level).
- **Range spread** — the width, expressed as ±% around the midpoint (commonly ±15–25%).

## Market positioning

Always state two things, or the band is a guess:

1. **Percentile** — where the midpoint sits in market data (e.g., **P50** = median, **P75** = upper quartile).
2. **Posture** — **lead** (pay ahead of market), **match** (at market), or **lag** (below, usually offset by
   equity/benefits). Posture is a deliberate strategy choice, not an accident.

## Compa-ratio

**Compa-ratio = actual pay ÷ band midpoint.**

| Compa-ratio | Reading |
|-------------|---------|
| ~0.85–0.90 | New in level / still ramping |
| ~1.00 | Fully proficient (at midpoint) |
| ~1.10–1.15 | Top of level / deep expertise |

Placement anchors on **level and skills** — never on prior salary, which imports historical bias.

## Compression & overlap

Adjacent levels' bands usually overlap (a top-of-level IC2 can earn near a new IC3). Healthy overlap is
fine; **compression** — new hires landing near tenured peers — is a retention bill paid later. Check it
when bands are set or the market moves fast.

## Guardrails (feed the equity-gate)

- Every number maps to **role, skills and impact** and a stated market reference.
- **Prior salary, negotiation strength and legacy title are not inputs** to placement.
- Bands and placements pass **`equity-gate`** before they're communicated.
- Jurisdiction-specific pay law gets legal/compliance review.

## Anti-patterns

- A band with no stated percentile/posture.
- Anchoring an offer on the candidate's last salary.
- Letting strong negotiators drift above peers for the same work.
- Ignoring compression until attrition spikes.
