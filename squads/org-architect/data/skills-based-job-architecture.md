# Skills-Based Job Architecture — Reference

Anchors the org-architect module. Based on Josh Bersin's job architecture work and the broader
skills-based organization movement (Deloitte, Mercer). Used by `role-designer` and `comp-strategist`.

## What it is

A **job architecture** is the shared backbone of role families, levels and the skills that define
each level. It replaces a sprawl of bespoke titles with a consistent grid that the whole employee
lifecycle hangs on: hiring (talent-compass), performance/leveling (performa), pay (comp-strategist)
and development (academy).

## Core principles

1. **Skills and outcomes over titles.** The work to be done and the skills to do it define the role —
   the title follows. Titles inflate; skills and outcomes don't.
2. **Levels are about the level of work**, not tenure. A level describes scope, autonomy and impact
   (e.g., "executes scoped problems independently" vs. "owns a domain"), not years served.
3. **Parallel tracks.** Individual-contributor (IC) and management (M) tracks are parallel and equally
   valued; advancement should not force a good IC into management to grow.
4. **Families group comparable work.** Role families (e.g., Data Analytics, Backend Eng) make pay
   benchmarking and equity comparisons meaningful — you compare like with like.

## The grid

| Element | Definition | Owner |
|---------|------------|-------|
| Role family | Group of roles sharing core skills | role-designer |
| Level (IC/M) | Level of work: scope, autonomy, impact | role-designer |
| Core skills | Skills that define competence at a level | role-designer |
| Band | Pay range mapped to a level + market | comp-strategist |

## Why it matters

- **Hiring:** a skills-based role yields a fair, evidence-based interview guide (talent-compass).
- **Pay equity:** consistent levels/families make unexplained gaps visible (equity-gate).
- **Mobility:** clear ladders make growth and internal moves legible to people.
- **4R:** a skills grid is what lets fourr-advisor size a gap in skills, not headcount.

## Anti-patterns

- Title-first design (the title exists, so we invent a role around it).
- Degree/tenure as a proxy for a demonstrable skill.
- One-off levels per team that can't be compared for equity.
- Forcing growth through management only.
